E4 · Test of Hypothesis

11 practice problems covering one sample z and t tests, two sample tests, the F test for variance, and the chi square test of independence. Problems E4.1 to E4.11.

Key Formulas · Test of Hypothesis
One sample z: \(z=\dfrac{\bar{x}-\mu_0}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}}\)   (use when \(\sigma\) known)
One sample t: \(t=\dfrac{\bar{x}-\mu_0}{s/\sqrt{n}}\), df \(=n-1\)   (use when \(\sigma\) unknown)
Two sample z: \(z=\dfrac{\bar{x}_1-\bar{x}_2}{\sqrt{s_1^2/n_1+s_2^2/n_2}}\)   (large samples)
Pooled variance: \(s_p^2=\dfrac{(n_1-1)s_1^2+(n_2-1)s_2^2}{n_1+n_2-2}\), df \(=n_1+n_2-2\)
Pooled t: \(t=\dfrac{\bar{x}_1-\bar{x}_2}{s_p\sqrt{1/n_1+1/n_2}}\)
F test (variances): \(F=s_1^2/s_2^2\), df \(=(n_1-1,\,n_2-1)\)
Chi-square: \(\chi^2=\sum\dfrac{(O-E)^2}{E}\), df \(=(r-1)(c-1)\), \(E_{ij}=R_iC_j/N\)
Tails: \(H_1:\mu\ne\mu_0\) two tailed; \(H_1:\mu\gt\mu_0\) right tailed; \(H_1:\mu\lt\mu_0\) left tailed
Decision: reject \(H_0\) if \(|t_\text{cal}|\gt t_\text{crit}\) (or \(p\lt\alpha\))
E4.1 How hypothesis tests help engineering decision making

Explain how hypothesis testing helps decision making in engineering, using a practical example involving an integrated circuit cutting machine.

Solution

Engineering decisions are made under uncertainty. A hypothesis test provides a disciplined, transparent procedure for deciding whether the evidence from a sample is strong enough to overturn a default assumption about a process, machine, or population parameter.

The five step procedure: (1) State \(H_0\) (status quo) and \(H_1\) (what to detect). (2) Choose a risk level \(\alpha\). (3) Compute the test statistic from the sample. (4) Compare with the critical value. (5) Make the decision in the language of the engineering problem.

EEE example (IC machine): A machine cuts square chips to 6 mm. Sample of 8: \(\bar{x}=6.001\) mm, \(s=0.001\) mm. Test: \(H_0:\mu=6\) vs \(H_1:\mu\ne6\) at \(\alpha=0.05\). \(t=2.828\gt t_{0.025,7}=2.365\): reject \(H_0\) · the machine needs resetting. The decision is documented and defensible.

CSE example (algorithm speed): A team claims a new caching strategy reduces mean page response time from 500 ms. They test 25 pages and find \(\bar{x}=480\) ms, \(s=40\) ms. One sample t test (\(H_0:\mu=500\), \(H_1:\mu\lt500\)) at \(\alpha=0.05\) gives \(t=(480-500)/(40/\sqrt{25})=-2.5\lt-t_{0.05,24}=-1.711\): reject \(H_0\). The caching strategy demonstrably reduces response time · an objective, documented result management can act on.

E4.2 Formulating hypotheses for a new production method

A company currently takes a mean of 220 seconds to assemble a component. A new method is expected to reduce assembly time. The new method will be adopted if a test at the 10% significance level supports it. Formulate the hypotheses and identify the test type.

Solution

\(H_0: \mu \ge 220\text{ seconds}\) (new method is no better)

\(H_1: \mu < 220\text{ seconds}\) (new method is faster)

This is a left tailed (one tailed) test at \(\alpha=0.10\). If the sample mean is significantly below 220, the test statistic falls in the left rejection region and the new method is adopted. The 10% significance level means we accept a 10% risk of switching to the new method even if it provides no real improvement.

E4.3 One sample t test · TV picture tube brightness

A design engineer claims a new TV picture tube needs on average more than 300 \(\mu\)A to reach required brightness. A sample of 10 tubes gives \(\bar{x}=317.2\;\mu\)A, \(s=15.7\;\mu\)A. Test at the 1% level.

Solution

\(H_0:\mu=300\) vs \(H_1:\mu\gt300\) (right tailed). \(n=10\), df=9, \(t_{0.01,9}=2.821\).

\[t = \frac{317.2-300}{15.7/\sqrt{10}} = \frac{17.2}{4.966} = 3.4644\]

\(t_\text{cal}=3.4644\gt2.821\): reject \(H_0\) (\(p=0.0036\)).

Conclusion: strong evidence that the mean current exceeds 300 \(\mu\)A. The engineer's claim is supported even at the strict 1% risk level.

Reject H₀. Mean current > 300 μA is supported at α = 1%.
E4.4 One sample t test · accuracy of an IC cutting machine

An IC manufacturer installed a machine to cut square ICs of 6 mm. A random sample of 8 ICs has mean 6.001 mm and standard deviation 0.001 mm. Test whether the machine was installed properly.

Solution

\(H_0:\mu=6\) vs \(H_1:\mu\ne6\) (two tailed). \(n=8\), df=7, \(t_{0.025,7}=2.365\).

\[t=\frac{6.001-6.000}{0.001/\sqrt{8}}=\frac{0.001}{0.000354}=2.828\]

\(|t|=2.828\gt2.365\): reject \(H_0\) at 5% (\(p=0.0255\)). The machine needs resetting.

Caveat: at \(\alpha=0.01\) (\(t_{0.005,7}=3.4995\)) the conclusion reverses. Also, the actual bias of 0.001 mm may be negligible in practice. Statistical significance \(\ne\) practical importance.

Reject H₀ at α = 5%. Machine is not installed properly (check practical significance).
E4.5 One sample z test · SSD response time

A company claims its new SSD has a mean response time of 0.5 ms with a standard deviation of 0.08 ms. A sample of 40 SSDs gives \(\bar{x}=0.48\) ms. Test the claim at the 1% risk level.

Solution

\(H_0:\mu=0.5\) vs \(H_1:\mu\ne0.5\) (two tailed). \(\sigma=0.08\) known, \(n=40\): z test. \(\pm z_{0.005}=\pm2.576\).

\[z=\frac{0.48-0.50}{0.08/\sqrt{40}}=\frac{-0.02}{0.01265}=-1.5811\]

\(|z|=1.581\lt2.576\): do not reject \(H_0\) (\(p=0.1138\)).

Conclusion: no significant evidence that the true mean differs from 0.5 ms. The company's claim is not contradicted. The shortfall of 0.02 ms is well within ordinary sampling variation for \(n=40\).

Do not reject H₀. Company's claim of 0.5 ms is not contradicted at α = 1%.
E4.6 One sample z test · energy consumption of vacuum cleaners

A manufacturer claims a vacuum cleaner uses on average 46 KWH or less per year, with known variability 2.5 KWH. A sample of 20 homes shows mean 44.5 KWH. Decide whether the cleaners meet the claim at the commonly used risk level.

Solution

\(H_0:\mu=46\) vs \(H_1:\mu\lt46\) (left tailed). \(\sigma=2.5\) known; at \(\alpha=0.05\): critical value \(-1.645\).

\[z=\frac{44.5-46}{2.5/\sqrt{20}}=\frac{-1.5}{0.559}=-2.683\]

\(z_\text{cal}=-2.683\lt-1.645\): reject \(H_0\) (\(p=0.0036\)).

Conclusion: mean consumption is significantly below 46 KWH. The cleaners comfortably meet the claim. The result holds even at \(\alpha=0.01\).

Reject H₀. Cleaners meet the claim (consumption significantly below 46 KWH).
E4.7 Two sample t test · assembling times of two working designs

23 workers using Design A: mean 300 s, sd 12 s. 25 workers using Design B: mean 335 s, sd 15 s. Is there a significant difference at the 1% level?

Solution

\(H_0:\mu_1=\mu_2\) vs \(H_1:\mu_1\ne\mu_2\). Pooled t test, df\(=46\), \(t_{0.005,46}=2.687\).

\[s_p^2=\frac{22(144)+24(225)}{46}=186.26,\quad s_p=13.648\]

\[t=\frac{300-335}{13.648\sqrt{1/23+1/25}}=\frac{-35}{3.943}=-8.876\]

\(|t|=8.876\gg2.687\): strongly reject \(H_0\). Design A is significantly faster, saving ~35 s per assembly.

Reject H₀. Design A is significantly faster at α = 1%.
E4.8 Two sample z test · lifetime of electronic chips from two brands

Brand 1: \(n=50\), mean 850 h, sd 25 h. Brand 2: \(n=50\), mean 910 h, sd 29 h. Test whether Brand 1 produces longer lasting chips.

Solution

\(H_0:\mu_1=\mu_2\) vs \(H_1:\mu_1\gt\mu_2\) (right tailed). Large samples: z test. At \(\alpha=0.05\): critical value \(+1.645\).

\[\text{SE}=\sqrt{625/50+841/50}=5.415,\quad z=\frac{850-910}{5.415}=-11.08\]

\(z_\text{cal}=-11.08\) is far from \(+1.645\): do not reject \(H_0\) in favour of \(H_1:\mu_1\gt\mu_2\). There is no evidence Brand 1 is better. On the contrary, Brand 2 is highly significantly better (two tailed \(|z|=11.08\gg1.96\)). Buy Brand 2.

Do not reject H₀ (Brand 1 better). Brand 2 is significantly superior.
E4.9 Two sample t test · strength of two types of copper wire

5 samples of Wire Type 1: mean 52 lb, sd 12 lb. 9 samples of Wire Type 2: mean 60 lb, sd 8 lb. Is there a significant difference in strength at the 5% level?

Solution

\(H_0:\mu_1=\mu_2\) vs \(H_1:\mu_1\ne\mu_2\). Pooled t, df\(=12\), \(t_{0.025,12}=2.179\).

\[s_p^2=\frac{4(144)+8(64)}{12}=90.667,\quad s_p=9.522\]

\[t=\frac{52-60}{9.522\sqrt{1/5+1/9}}=\frac{-8}{5.311}=-1.506\]

\(|t|=1.506\lt2.179\): do not reject \(H_0\). The 8 lb gap can be explained by chance with these small, variable samples. More samples are recommended before concluding Type 2 is stronger.

Do not reject H₀. No significant strength difference at α = 5%.
E4.10 F test then t test · comparing two filling machines

Machine 1: \(n_1=22\), \(\bar{x}_1=5.90\) kg, \(s_1=2.00\) kg. Machine 2: \(n_2=20\), \(\bar{x}_2=6.28\) kg, \(s_2=1.89\) kg. Decide whether there is a significant difference between the machines.

Solution

Part 1 · F test (variances). \(F=4.00/3.5721=1.120\), df\(=(21,19)\). At \(\alpha=0.05\): \(F_{0.025}(21,19)=2.49\). Since \(1.120\lt2.49\): variances not significantly different · equal variance assumption is valid.

Part 2 · Pooled t test (means). df\(=40\), \(t_{0.025,40}=2.021\).

\[s_p^2=\frac{21(4.00)+19(3.5721)}{40}=3.797,\quad s_p=1.948\]

\[t=\frac{5.90-6.28}{1.948\sqrt{1/22+1/20}}=\frac{-0.38}{0.602}=-0.631\]

\(|t|=0.631\lt2.021\): do not reject.

Conclusion: no significant difference in either mean or variability of bag weights. The machines are equivalent; no adjustment is justified.

Both tests: do not reject H₀. Machines are equivalent at α = 5%.
E4.11 Chi square test of independence · chip grade vs manufacturing plant

Three plants A, B, C produce chips classified as First, Second, or Third grade:

First620750400
Second130200140
Third504050

Test whether chip quality depends on the manufacturing plant.

Solution

\(H_0\): grade is independent of plant. \(H_1\): grade depends on plant.

Marginal totals: Row: 1770, 470, 140. Column: 800, 990, 590. \(N=2380\).

Expected frequencies \(E_{ij}=R_iC_j/N\):

ABC
First594.96736.26438.78
Second157.98195.50116.51
Third47.0658.2434.71

\(\chi^2=\sum(O-E)^2/E=27.17\)

df\(=(3-1)(3-1)=4\). \(\chi^2_{0.05,4}=9.488\); \(\chi^2_{0.01,4}=13.277\). Since \(27.17\gg13.277\): reject \(H_0\) at both 5% and 1% (\(p\lt0.0001\)).

Conclusion: chip quality does depend on the plant. Plant C produces far fewer first grade chips than expected and is the weakest; Plant B is the strongest. Investigate Plant C's process.

Reject H₀. χ² = 27.17 ≫ 9.488. Quality depends on plant; Plant C is the weakest.
